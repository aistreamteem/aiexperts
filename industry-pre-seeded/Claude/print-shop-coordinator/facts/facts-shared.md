### 2026-11-08: Bindery / finishing
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/print-shop-coordinator/docs/facts/bindery.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Bindery is the finishing work after print: cut, fold, stitch, bind, laminate, drill, number, insert, or kit. Typical US SMB shops keep a bindery list or a second calendar because the folder can sit while the press is free. Copy bindery steps from the ticket in their order. Do not skip a step to make due-today look possible. Do not invent a bindery hour or a hand-work rate. If the bindery list and the ticket disagree, quote both. Outside bindery is a vendor note on their file, not a payment you send. Quality checks (page order, trim, stitch placement) follow their checklist if they stored one. A job can be press-complete and bindery-not-started; say that on the board. You do not hide a bindery backlog inside “on press.” They sequence the floor. Starter until they teach *this* shop's bindery queue words.

### 2026-11-08: Bleed
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/print-shop-coordinator/docs/facts/bleed.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Bleed is image that extends past trim so the cutter does not leave a white sliver. Typical US SMB checklists ask for a house bleed (often one-eighth inch) and a safety for type. Copy their number. If bleed is missing on the file, ask; do not invent a bleed by stretching art unless their checklist says they may. Do not shrink trim to fake a bleed. Crop marks are not bleed. A full-bleed piece with no bleed is a preflight fail on most house lists. You do not change finished size to dodge the fail. Gang and imposition must still honor their bleed. You do not hide a no-bleed file inside “will cut carefully.” They ask the customer for a new file or they waive in writing. Safety is not bleed; keep both rows if they have both. Starter until they teach *this* shop's bleed and safety.

### 2026-11-08: Change after proof
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/print-shop-coordinator/docs/facts/change-after-proof.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

A change after proof is new copy, size, color, or quantity after they already approved or after a proof went out. Typical US SMB shops call these author alterations and want a new proof unless they waive in writing. Do not apply the change to the press file and keep the old approval. Do not invent a due date that assumes the change is free. Reprint-reason text is required if the change is a shop error versus a customer change — use their words, do not invent blame. Quantity or stock changes also hit inventory. You do not hide the change inside the old job number if they issue a revision. Billing of AAs is their estimate file. You still do not give copyright advice about new art. They approve the new proof. Starter until they teach how *this* shop revisions tickets.

### 2026-11-08: Coating and varnish
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/print-shop-coordinator/docs/facts/coating.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Coating is aqueous, UV, varnish, or a spot varnish they named, often written next to color. Typical US SMB tickets say flood AQ gloss or matte, or UV on the face only. Copy the coating and the sides. Do not add a coat to protect a job you worry about. Do not swap UV for AQ because the coater is free. Coating can change dry time and bindery start; still do not invent a due date. Customer-furnished stock is paper or substrate the customer sent. Typical US SMB shops receipt it on a CFS note: count, size, weight, damage, and who owns leftover. Label it CFS. **Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/print-shop-coordinator/docs/facts/customer-po.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

The customer's purchase order is their authorization to print, not a payment you take and not a card authorization. ### 2026-11-08: Digital versus offset
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/print-shop-coordinator/docs/facts/digital-vs-offset.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Digital versus offset is the process the ticket already chose, usually from quantity, versioning, or color needs they priced. Typical US SMB shops break around a few hundred to a few thousand, but that break is theirs, not a rule you apply. Copy the process from the ticket. Do not flip a job to digital to hit a due date their offset calendar missed. Do not flip to offset to “look better” without their change. ### 2026-11-08: How this role files work items
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/print-shop-coordinator/docs/facts/how-we-file.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Day-to-day work does **not** go in one shared pile. Each **work item** gets its own folder under `work-items/<id>/` so Tuesday does not overwrite Monday, and one ticket does not mix with the next.

Typical name for *this* role (until they remember a different pattern):

- **What one folder is:** print job number or board day
- **How to name it:** `work-items/WID-20260915/` (or their MIS id if they prefer)
- **Pattern:** `work-items/<id>/`

Inside every Work Item folder:

- `in/` — files they dropped for *this* item only
- `work/` — scratch while this item is in progress
- `out/` — the finished list or pack for *this* item
- `evidence/` — check logs for this item only

Proofs and stock for that ticket stay in that folder. A day board is a separate named Work Item.

If they already file another way (their software’s id, a job number, a period name), **use their words**. Ask before starting if it is unclear which item this is. Never dump new files into yesterday’s folder. Never put two tickets, two board days, or two periods in the same Work Item folder unless they asked for a combined pack and named it that way (for example one day-list).

**Status:** active

The job number is the shop's unique handle for one ticket, one reprint, or one split of a ticket if they number splits. ### 2026-11-08: Special-order stock
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/print-shop-coordinator/docs/facts/special-order-stock.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Special-order stock is paper they do not keep on the house list: a mill make, a color, a size, or a certified sheet. Typical US SMB tickets mark SO or “order paper” and point at a mill quote. Copy the mill item and the quoted lead time from that file. Do not treat special-order as on-hand. Do not invent a lead time. Do not substitute house stock to dodge the wait unless they asked. If the mill quote expired, ask before anyone uses the price or the date. Customer-furnished is not special-order. You do not pay the mill. Due dates on jobs that need special-order paper must survive the mill date plus their in-plant calendar — never invent a due that ignores both. They decide whether to wait. Partial mill receipts still leave the rest special-order. Starter until they teach how *this* shop flags special-order.

### 2026-11-08: Split shipment
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/print-shop-coordinator/docs/facts/split-ship.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

A split ship is more than one delivery or pickup against one ordered quantity: a partial now and a balance later, or two ship-to addresses. Typical US SMB tickets write split qty and each method. Copy those lines. Do not invent a split to make due-today look complete. Do not hide the balance. Each drop needs its own delivery ticket. Overs, if any, follow their file on the last drop or as they wrote. You do not promise the balance date without the calendar. Customer PO lines that already split should match the ticket; if not, ask. You do not store PAN on either drop. Bindery may finish in waves; say which qty is ready. They tell the customer. Carton counts belong on each drop, not only the first. Pickup versus freight can differ by drop. Starter until they teach how *this* shop writes splits.

### 2026-11-08: Stitch or bind
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/print-shop-coordinator/docs/facts/stitch.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Stitch or bind is how pages become a book: saddle stitch, side stitch, perfect bind, coil, wire-o, or paste. Typical US SMB shops write stitch count, spine width, or coil color when those fields exist. Copy the bind type from the ticket. Do not invent a page count to make a saddle-stitch possible. Do not swap perfect bind for stitch to hit a due date. Cover and text stocks must match their ticket; a mismatch is a stock question. Creep, grind-off, and hinge score are their bindery notes if present — do not invent them. If the estimate says saddle and the ticket says perfect, list both. Outside bindery still needs their PO to that vendor; you do not pay it. You do not hide a missing cover file inside “ready to stitch.” Starter until they teach *this* shop's bind vocabulary.

### 2026-11-08: Stock on hand
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/print-shop-coordinator/docs/facts/stock-on-hand.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Stock on hand is the sheet, roll, or digital-media count on their stock CSV or inventory export, not a walk of the skid you invent. Typical US SMB shops export from the MIS or keep a workbook by mill item and house alias. Copy on-hand, reserved, and unit as they coded them. Compare on-hand to the pull qty implied by the ticket and their imposition file. Shortages stay visible; do not hide a hole by substituting a close sheet. Do not invent a count. Customer-furnished stock is not on-hand mill stock unless they receipted it that way. Aged or damaged rows stay as they flagged them. You do not pay a mill to refill. You do not promise the floor that a short sheet will arrive tomorrow without their mill-order date. Starter until they teach how *this* shop counts and reserves paper.

### 2026-11-08: Waste and spoilage
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/print-shop-coordinator/docs/facts/waste.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Waste is sheets that never became shippable pieces: make-ready, running waste, bindery trim, or spoilage they recorded. Typical US SMB shops keep a waste percent on the estimate or a count on the ticket after the run. Copy their number. Do not invent a waste percent to explain a short ship. Do not hide a shortage by raising waste. Press count minus waste should reconcile to bindery or ship count if they reconcile; if the three disagree, list all three. Plate or ink waste is still not a mill payment. You do not promise a yield. Environmental or scrap-bin notes stay as their file. A make-good reprint is a new ticket, not a waste line you grow. They decide whether waste is billed. Overs that shipped are not waste. Starter until they teach how *this* shop records waste versus overs.
