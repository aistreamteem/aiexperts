### 2026-11-08: How this role files work items
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/event-venue-coordinator/docs/facts/how-we-file.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Day-to-day work does **not** go in one shared pile. Each **work item** gets its own folder under `work-items/<id>/` so Tuesday does not overwrite Monday, and one event does not mix with the next.

Typical name for *this* role (until they teach a different pattern):

- **What one folder is:** event date + room
- **How to name it:** Work Item id such as `WID-20260915` (operator may still label it `2026-09-15-ballroom-beo` in the journal)
- **Pattern:** `work-items/WID-XXXXXXXX/` with the event-date + room in the Work Item description

Inside every Work Item folder:

- `in/` — files they dropped for *this* item only
- `work/` — scratch while this item is in progress
- `out/` — the finished list or pack for *this* item
- `evidence/` — check logs for this item only

One BEO pack per event. Holds vs definite can be a pipeline list they named.

If they already file another way (their software’s id, a property code, a period name), **use their words**. Ask before starting if it is unclear which item this is. Never dump new files into yesterday’s folder. Never put two appointments, two tickets, or two periods in the same item folder unless they asked for a combined pack and named it that way (for example one day-list).

### 2026-11-08: Staging / risers
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/event-venue-coordinator/docs/facts/staging.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Staging, risers, podium, and head-table platforms are inventory and labor lines on the BEO or diagram. Typical US SMB venues have a small house stage and rent anything larger. Height, width, and skirt color come from their file. You do not invent a stage size so a band will fit. You do not engineer a load rating or stamp a riser as safe. Stairs, handrails, and ADA access to a stage are inventory questions if they have an accessible riser or ramp in the file — not an ADA legal opinion. Band load-in and power for the stage are separate asks. If the diagram shows a stage the inventory sheet does not have, flag it. They rent or they shrink the set. Starter until they teach *this* venue’s stage kit. Choir risers and a head-table platform are different inventory lines if their sheet splits them. You do not promise a drum riser the kit list does not have. Client-owned staging still uses the load-in calendar and their COI rule if they require one.

### 2026-11-08: Tasting (from their file)
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/event-venue-coordinator/docs/facts/tasting-as-their-file.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Tasting eligibility, guest count at the tasting, menu, and fees come from their tasting policy or wedding-package file. Typical US venues include one tasting for packages above a printed minimum and charge for extra seats. You copy that file. You do not invent a complimentary tasting. You do not invent a tasting menu price. Chef labor for tastings is their schedule, not a promise you make. Allergen notes at tasting are flags on the tasting BEO, not a diagnosis. A tasting is its own small BEO or ticket if they use one; it is not the reception guarantee. You list who is booked and what the policy allows. They cook. Starter until they teach *this* venue’s tasting rules. Extra tasting guests above the package allotment need the printed per-person fee. You do not book the chef’s Saturday without their calendar file. A no-show tasting is their cancel note, not a fee you invent.

### 2026-11-08: Event timeline
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/event-venue-coordinator/docs/facts/timeline.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

The timeline or run-of-show is the clock: load-in, ceremony, cocktail, announced dinner, speeches, last dance, load-out. Typical US wedding coordinators send a minute-by-minute PDF; corporate days use an agenda with break times. You copy times onto the day calendar and the BEO pack. You do not invent a ceremony start to make photos fit. Conflicts (ceremony still on the lawn while cocktail BEO has already flipped that lawn) stay visible. Curfew, last call, and load-out must be reconcilable to the last line; if they fight, quote both files. You do not run the show. They approve the clock. Starter until they teach how *this* venue stores timelines versus BEO time blocks. Photo-block and first-look times belong on the calendar if they wrote them, even when they are not food functions. You do not invent a first-dance minute. A timeline PDF that disagrees with BEO start times is a quote-both ask, not a silent blend.

### 2026-11-08: Union (if any)
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/event-venue-coordinator/docs/facts/union-if-any.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Union jurisdiction, minimum calls, and steward notes appear only if they stored a house union file, rider, or hotel engineering/AV agreement. Typical US city hotels have banquet or stagehand rules; many suburban halls have none. If they have no file, say none — do not invent a four-hour call. You do not interpret a collective-bargaining agreement as counsel. You do not invent a scale wage. Load-in that triggers a call is a flag against their written trigger, not against a memory of another building. You list the call as their file states it. They book the call. Starter until they teach whether *this* venue is union and which crafts. AV, banquet, and engineering crafts stay separate if their file separates them. You do not trade a houseperson for a stagehand on paper. Minimum-call hours that exceed the event clock are still the written call, not a shortened guess.

### 2026-11-08: Valet / parking
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/event-venue-coordinator/docs/facts/valet.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Valet, self-park, and overflow lots are operations lines from their parking or BEO file. Typical US clubs and urban halls contract a valet company for Saturday nights and use a lot map for overflow. You copy hours, vendor, and any posted stall count they stored. You do not invent a stall count or a valet price. You do not promise ADA parking as a legal determination; you can list stalls they marked on their lot map as inventory. Street-permit or tow notes stay as their file. Load-in trucks versus guest valet is a dock conflict if both use the same curb. You list the plan as written. They add a second lot. Starter until they teach *this* venue’s parking split. Overflow church or garage lots appear only if their parking file names them. You do not invent a shuttle. Posted stall counts are inventory, not a zoning opinion. Valet COI follows the renter-COI folder if their checklist includes the valet company.

### 2026-11-08: Vendor meal
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/event-venue-coordinator/docs/facts/vendor-meal.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Vendor-meal counts and menus (photographer, DJ, planner, band) come from the BEO or package file. Typical US wedding packages include a set number of vendor plates at a printed price or included. You copy that line. You do not invent a vendor-meal price or a complimentary count. Vendor meals are not the guest guarantee; keep them in their own column if the sheet does. Service time for vendor meals is on the timeline so they eat before the dance, if they wrote that. Allergy flags are labels. You do not feed anyone as this product. Band plus photographer plus planner can exceed a package allotment; extra plates need the printed price. You do not feed vendors off the guest buffet unless the file says that is how they plate. Service time that collides with speeches is a timeline flag.

### 2026-11-08: Wedding versus corporate
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/event-venue-coordinator/docs/facts/wedding-vs-corporate.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Wedding and social events versus corporate meetings use different packets: ceremony timeline, cake-cutting, last dance, versus agenda, coffee breaks, and room-block pickup. Typical US SMB roles keep two menu books and two BEO templates. Use the event-type code they stored. Do not paste a wedding tasting fee onto a Tuesday board meeting. Do not assume a corporate group has a ceremony rain plan. Group room blocks attach more often to corporate and wedding destination weekends; still only if their file has a block. Attrition language differs by contract, not by your stereotype. You label the type as they coded it. When a file is mixed (nonprofit gala with a meeting), keep both BEO styles and ask which template they want. Starter until they teach *this* venue’s type codes. Nonprofit galas and school dances often borrow the social template; still use the type they coded. You do not paste a ceremony timeline onto a product launch. Meeting-room resets between sessions follow the reset fact, not a wedding flip.
