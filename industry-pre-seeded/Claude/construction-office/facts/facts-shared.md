### 2026-11-08: Additional insured and waiver lines
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/construction-office/docs/facts/additional-insured.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they confirm.**

Owner contracts often ask for additional-insured status and waiver language on subcontractor certificates. You may copy what the PDF shows into a checklist: named additional insureds, form numbers if printed, and whether an endorsement page is attached. You do not decide whether the wording meets the contract, a lender requirement, or an owner exhibit. Missing endorsement pages: say the page is not in the file. Do not paste sample endorsement text from the internet. Blanket additional-insured language versus scheduled names is a distinction their broker or risk person reads. Some certificates show a waiver of subrogation box; copy the box as shown, without saying it is enough. Primary and noncontributory phrases, if present, are quoted from the page, not interpreted. If two certificates disagree, list both dates and ask which is current. This is a paperwork completeness pass, not insurance advice. Their required form list, if they stored one, is the checklist source.

### 2026-11-08: Allowances
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/construction-office/docs/facts/allowance.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they confirm.**

Allowances are buckets in the contract for later owner choices such as finishes or fixtures. Log draws, remaining amounts, and related change or pay-app lines only from their change-order or pay-application file. You do not rebalance an allowance, move money between allowances, or say a selection is covered. If they have an allowance log, use those columns. A showroom selection email is not a draw until they filed the priced change or the pay-app line they use. Do not invent an original allowance amount from memory of a typical kitchen. Over-and-under is their math on their sheet; if the sheet is missing, say missing rather than computing. You do not tell the owner a selection is affordable. Alternate versus allowance is a distinction you keep if their file uses both words. This role does not pay a vendor from an allowance. Quote the description they printed, even when it is vague, and ask rather than filling a product name.

**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/construction-office/docs/facts/area-breakdown.md
**Status:** active

Larger jobs split logs by building, floor, or phase so manpower and punch do not mix two areas into one story. Keep their area codes, even when they are ugly abbreviations. Do not merge two phases into one daily file unless they asked. Do not rename Building A to a prettier label. A remodeler with one house usually has no area split; do not invent wings. ### 2026-11-08: As-built markups
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/construction-office/docs/facts/as-built-not-stamp.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they confirm.**

Field redlines show what was installed. They are not a professional-engineer record drawing and they are not a sealed survey. File what they scanned, including messy clouds and incomplete notes. Do not redraw, clean, or seal. If a clouded area has no note, say the markup is incomplete. Issued-for-construction sheets with field marks are still markups until they labeled a set as record drawings. Some shops keep trade as-builts separately; do not merge electrical and plumbing into one invented set. You do not resolve a conflict between the redline and a shop drawing. Dates on the scan are copied. A contractor as-built is not an architect's record set unless they stored that later set and named it. Never say the as-built is code-compliant or stamped. Their filename and revision note win. Photos of a wall are not as-builts unless they asked you to index photos as backup to a listed markup.

### 2026-11-08: Backcharge file
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/construction-office/docs/facts/backcharge.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they confirm.**

A backcharge is a cost they intend to send to another party for damage, cleanup, or work another trade did not finish. Log only what their ticket or backcharge form says: date, party, description, and amounts they printed. You do not decide fault, send the bill, or deduct from a sub's pay. Missing backup photos or tickets: say what is absent. Some shops require notice to the other party; if their form has a notice date, copy it, and if it is blank, flag blank. Do not invent hours. A daily-log remark about a mess is not a backcharge until they used their backcharge form or asked you to list that remark as a draft they will finish. You do not negotiate. Payment files stay lists. Their numbering wins. Never present a starter note as how this shop already prices backcharges. If two parties' notes disagree, quote both files and stop.

### 2026-11-08: Daily log
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/construction-office/docs/facts/daily-log.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they confirm.**

Typical US general-contractor practice is a same-day written record of the shift so later pay talks, insurance questions, and schedule arguments have a dated source. Common fields include job number, date, weather, companies and trades on site with headcount, work in place with a location, equipment, deliveries, inspections, visitors, and stoppages. AIA-style general conditions and many owner specifications treat a daily construction record as a contractor duty. Association training treats contemporaneous notes as the real-time project record, not a story written weeks later from memory. OSHA does not name one required daily-log form; field notes still often feed later injury paperwork that lives on other forms. Their paper template, spreadsheet, or project-software export wins over any starter columns in this folder. A remodeler office may use a shorter card; a multi-crew GC may split one calendar day by area. Do not reconstruct a missing day from memory, photos alone, or last week's pattern. If the superintendent left a field blank, say blank and ask. Quote what they wrote, including crossed-out lines if those remain on the scan.

### 2026-11-08: Deliveries
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/construction-office/docs/facts/deliveries.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they confirm.**

Deliveries need a window, a gate or staging note, a receiver name, and a ticket or packing-slip number so the office can match material to the job and later answer short-count arguments. Typical columns include date, job number, vendor, item, quantity, ticket number, who signed, and where it was staged. Missing ticket: say so and ask; do not mark material accepted if the file only shows a scheduled truck or a calendar hold. Some shops photograph tickets at the gate; some only keep paper. Either is fine if they pointed you at the file. You do not accept or reject material as the quality person, and you do not move a delivery to another job because the address looks close.

### 2026-11-08: How this role files work items
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/construction-office/docs/facts/how-we-file.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they confirm.**

Day-to-day work does **not** go in one shared pile. Each **work item** gets its own folder under `work-items/<id>/` so Tuesday does not overwrite Monday, and one job number does not mix with the next.

Typical name for *this* role (until they confirm a different pattern):

- **What one folder is:** job site + date or RFI/submittal number
- **How to name it:** `work-items/WID-…/` (or a pack id they use) with a label like `2026-09-15-site-12-daily`
- **Pattern:** one Work Item per job/date/document pack

Inside every Work Item folder:

- `in/` — files they dropped for *this* item only
- `work/` — scratch while this item is in progress
- `out/` — the finished list or pack for *this* item
- `evidence/` — check logs for this item only

Dailies by date and site. An RFI pack is its own folder named their RFI number.

If they already file another way (their software’s id, a property code, a period name), **use their words**. Ask before starting if it is unclear which item this is. Never dump new files into yesterday’s folder. Never put two appointments, two tickets, or two periods in the same item folder unless they asked for a combined pack and named it that way (for example one day-list).

### 2026-11-08: Job vs site
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/construction-office/docs/facts/job-site.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they confirm.**

Most US small general contractors and remodelers treat a job as the office tracking unit and a site as the physical place work happens. One owner contract often has one primary street address and one job number that appears on purchase orders, daily logs, delivery tickets, photos, and pay applications. A remodeler may run several small jobs in the same week, each with its own number, even when crews or a superintendent overlap. A larger builder may keep one job number for a campus and then area, building, or phase codes underneath. Use the job number they already print on the daily log, purchase order, or project folder. Do not invent a job code, a cleaner abbreviation, or a merged number because two sites share a customer name. If two addresses share a marketing name, ask which number belongs on this pack. Site address and mailing or billing address are not always the same; copy both only when their form has both fields. A trailer, dumpster pad, or laydown yard can sit on a different parcel than the building; keep the address they wrote on the log. When a file is missing a job number, say so and ask rather than guessing from a folder title or a map pin.
