### 2026-11-08: ADAS as their operation, not a calibrate stamp
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/auto-body-estimator/docs/facts/adas-as-their-op-not-calibrate-stamp.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

ADAS lines are operations their estimate or procedure file already listed — static or dynamic calibration, aim, or scan as they coded — not a stamp that sensors are calibrated or that the car is safe to drive. Typical US collision shops add ADAS after a panel, glass, or structure line when their OEM procedure PDF or their estimating add says so. Copy the operation and any sublet quote they filed. Never invent ADAS hours or a calibration price. Never certify that radar, camera, or lidar is in spec. NHTSA driver-assistance pages are orientation only. If the procedure PDF is missing and the estimate has ADAS, flag missing procedure; do not invent the method. Mechanical scan tools on a service RO are a different role unless they attached that print to this claim. This Expert lists ADAS ops and missing prints. They or a sublet calibrate. Starter until they teach which *this* shop calibrates in-house versus sublet.

### 2026-11-08: Insurer assignment
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/auto-body-estimator/docs/facts/assignment.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

An assignment is the insurer's or customer's written referral of one loss to this shop, not a mechanical repair order and not a promise that the car will be repaired here. Typical US collision offices receive it as a CCC, Mitchell, Audatex, or DRP portal PDF, a fax, or an email they saved: claim number, VIN, date of loss, insured or claimant name, deductible if shown, rental flag, and sometimes a preferred-shop code. Copy those fields. Do not invent an assignment so the board looks full. Do not treat a walk-in estimate as an assignment unless they filed it that way. A mechanical service advisor's RO is a different role; this file is a claim handle, not a customer-states concern. If two assignments share a VIN, list both and ask which is live. Missing assignment on an insurer-pay file is an ask, not a guess. This Expert lists intake. They accept or decline the job. Starter until they teach how *this* shop files assignments and what their DRP screen requires.

### 2026-11-08: How this role files work items
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/auto-body-estimator/docs/facts/how-we-file.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Day-to-day work does **not** go in one shared pile. Each **work item** gets its own folder under `work-items/<id>/` so Tuesday does not overwrite Monday, and one assignment does not mix with the next.

Typical name for *this* role (until they confirm a different pattern):

- **What one folder is:** repair assignment / RO / claim pack
- **How to name it:** `work-items/2026-09-15-ro-2104/`
- **Pattern:** `work-items/YYYY-MM-DD-ro-or-claim/` or `work-items/WID-XXXXXXXX/`

Inside every item folder:

- `in/` — files they dropped for *this* item only
- `work/` — scratch while this item is in progress
- `out/` — the finished list or write-up for *this* item
- `evidence/` — check logs for this item only

Photos and supplements stay in that RO folder. Prefer RO or claim number, not VIN in the folder name if they use a shorter id.

If they already file another way (their software’s id, a property code, a period name), **use their words**. Ask before starting if it is unclear which item this is. Never dump new files into yesterday’s folder. Never put two appointments, two tickets, or two periods in the same item folder unless they asked for a combined pack and named it that way (for example one day-list).
