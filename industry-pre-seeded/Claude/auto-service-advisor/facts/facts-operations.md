## 2026-11-08: Additional findings
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/auto-service-advisor/docs/facts/additional-findings.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

When the technician finds work beyond the original authorized scope, typical US independent and dealer practice is to stop, present findings in plain language, quote options from their labor matrix and parts file, and get new authorization before proceeding. Do not add the new line to sold until their auth record exists. Keep the original concern visible so the extra work is not mistaken for the reason they came in. MPI red items and related services are recommendations with their prices, not a legal requirement and not a diagnosis you invent. You do not decide that aftermarket is unsafe compared with OEM; use the quality and price they quoted. If they present good better best, copy those three from their sheet, do not invent a fourth. Waiters and warranty cars still need the new approve. The findings list exists so the advisor can call from a complete pack. Ask when a finding has no price in their files.

**Source:** seeds/auto-service-advisor/docs/facts/comeback.md

Do not bury the flag inside a routine oil-change write-up. Do not write that the last tech was wrong. If they have a comeback reason code, copy it. Typical US role practice until they teach how this shop codes callbacks versus new work.

## 2026-11-08: Concern in customer words
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/auto-service-advisor/docs/facts/concern.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

The concern, often labeled complaint or customer states on US repair orders, is what the customer reported in their words plus conditions that help a technician try to duplicate it. Typical write-up adds when it happens, how often, speed, gear, hot or cold, weather, load, and left or right if they said so. Independent and dealer roles both treat this line as the record of the request, not a diagnosis. Write Customer states and keep the symptom. A concern is not your guess at a failed part, a TSB, or a correction. ASE-style service writing keeps the customer sentence even after the tech later writes a cause. If they only said check engine or noise, keep that thin wording and list missing conditions as questions. Do not replace their words with a cleaner shop phrase that names a part. The pack exists so the tech and the invoice still show what was asked. This is typical US counter practice until they teach their exact concern field and any required condition checklist.

## 2026-11-08: How this role files work items
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/auto-service-advisor/docs/facts/how-we-file.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Day-to-day work does **not** go in one shared pile. Each **work item** gets its own folder under `work-items/<id>/` so Tuesday does not overwrite Monday, and one repair order does not mix with the next.

Typical name for *this* role (until they teach a different pattern):

- **What one folder is:** repair order
- **How to name it:** `work-items/2026-09-15-ro-5521/`
- **Pattern:** `work-items/YYYY-MM-DD-ro/` or `work-items/WID-XXXXXXXX/`

Inside every Work Item folder:

- `in/` — files they dropped for *this* item only
- `work/` — scratch while this item is in progress
- `out/` — the finished list or write-up for *this* item
- `evidence/` — check logs for this item only

Estimates, photos, and status for that RO stay together. Do not drop a second car into the same folder.

If they already file another way (their software’s id, a property code, a period name), **use their words**. Ask before starting if it is unclear which item this is. Never dump new files into yesterday’s folder. Never put two appointments, two tickets, or two periods in the same item folder unless they asked for a combined pack and named it that way (for example one day-list).

**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/auto-service-advisor/docs/facts/tsb.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Technical service bulletins and campaigns appear only if their repair-info printout or OEM portal screenshot is in the file. You do not browse a paywalled manual into docs. You do not declare a recall is open. List TSB number in their printout or ask. Independent roles see TSBs when someone printed them from Mitchell, ALLDATA, or similar; dealer roles live in campaign screens. Either way, the saved page is the source. A bulletin is not a diagnosis you apply, not a promise of coverage, and not a safety ruling you add. Do not rename a customer concern to match a bulletin title. If they have a campaign number on the RO, copy it; if they do not, do not invent one from a public recall list. The printout exists so the tech and warranty clerk can see what this shop already pulled. Starter practice until they teach who checks campaigns at this counter.

## 2026-11-08: VIN handling
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/auto-service-advisor/docs/facts/vin.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

VIN is copied from their RO, a door-jamb photo they saved, or the shop software field. Do not invent a VIN. Do not fix a check digit. A public decoder such as NHTSA vPIC is only useful after they gave you the VIN; it does not replace their RO identity. Decode results are manufacturer-reported build data, not a diagnosis and not a parts supersession you invent. Independent and dealer roles both need VIN for parts and history; dealers often require it before warranty dispatch. If the scanned RO and the export disagree, ask which VIN is live. Do not paste extra owner personal data into docs. Seventeen characters is typical for light vehicles they write up this way; if they stored a serial on equipment, copy that field name. VIN exists so labor guide, parts, and campaigns can be matched later. Typical practice until they teach where this shop captures VIN at write-up.

## 2026-11-08: Holds: auth vs parts vs tech
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/auto-service-advisor/docs/facts/wait-auth-parts.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

A ticket can wait on authorization, parts, or technician time. Use their hold reason. Mixing waiting parts with waiting auth hides the next action. If both are true, list both from the file. Independent and dealer boards both fail when a tile says waiting without a reason. A vendor ETA explains a parts hold only when their confirmation has one. An empty auth log explains an auth hold. Tech time is their roster or board, not your guess that a bay is free. Holds are not a diagnosis and not a safe-to-drive opinion. Do not clear a hold in the pack because you think the part arrived. Special-order and backorder stay parts holds until their status changes in the file. The reason exists so the open-RO list tells the advisor what to do next. Starter until they teach this shop's hold codes.

## 2026-11-08: Waiter vs drop-off
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/auto-service-advisor/docs/facts/waiter-vs-drop.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Waiters and drop-offs have different promised times and often different loaner or shuttle needs. Copy their flag. Do not move a drop-off to waiter status in the pack. If the file is silent, ask. Independent counters feel this split all morning; dealer lanes do too, with more loaner traffic. A waiter still needs a written concern, auth for sold lines, and parts status from files. A drop-off still needs a promised window they wrote, not one you invent. Additional findings on a waiter need a fast auth record just like a drop-off; do not skip the who when amount. Waiter is not a diagnosis and not a reason to skip MPI if their process requires it. If they cap waiter jobs by hour or by job type, quote that rule from their file. The flag exists so the board and the loaner log stay honest. Starter until they teach this shop's waiter rules.

## 2026-11-08: Oil, tire, and waste tickets
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/auto-service-advisor/docs/facts/waste-tickets.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Waste-oil and scrap-tire tickets are vendor documents they keep. You may list ticket dates from those PDFs. You do not certify environmental compliance. Do not store extra generator IDs in docs unless they asked. Independent and dealer shops both have pickup receipts; the role job is completeness of the file they named, not a legal stamp. Waste notes are not a diagnosis and not a reason to invent a hazmat fee. If a fee on the RO points at a ticket that is missing, ask. Do not interpret EPA or state rules. Do not paste customer names onto a waste log. Do not treat a missing ticket as proof the shop is out of compliance. The tickets exist so their office can find the pickup later. Starter practice until they teach which waste vendors this shop uses and which fields they want on a list.

## 2026-11-08: Year, make, model, plate
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/auto-service-advisor/docs/facts/ymm.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Year, make, model, engine, and plate if they use plate must match the RO. If the export and the scanned RO disagree, ask which is correct. Do not pick an engine code, displacement, or drive type from memory. Trim and engine matter for parts and book time; only use what their file shows. Independent counters sometimes write the plate and a short YMM; dealer DMS rows usually lock VIN-decoded YMM and still need a human check when the customer swapped plates. A wrong engine on the ticket is a parts error waiting to happen, not something you silently correct. Do not invent a Canadian or fleet unit number. If they store production date or paint code, copy only from their field. YMM exists so the estimate and the parts PO describe the same vehicle. Starter practice until they teach this shop's required vehicle fields at open.
