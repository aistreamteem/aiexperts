## 2026-11-08: Access is an ask; codes stay out of docs
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/pest-control-office/docs/facts/access-gate-not-in-docs.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Access is whether the tech can get onto the property: gate, lockbox, HOA clicker, dock hours, or a dog that must be inside. Typical US commercial and gated-community pest routes fail when the office forgets a window or a holiday gate. Ask whether access is solved. Never store a gate code, lockbox combo, alarm PIN, or door code in this folder, in docs/, or in a write-up. If their export already dumped a code into a CSV, do not copy it forward; say access notes exist in their system and they must handle codes there. Time windows and “call ahead” flags are fine to list. School and food docks often have windows; copy those. You flag missing access. They keep secrets in their own lockbox. Starter until they teach how *this* shop shares clickers without writing numbers into the Expert folder.

## 2026-11-08: How this role files work items
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/pest-control-office/docs/facts/how-we-file.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Day-to-day work does **not** go in one shared pile. Each **work item** gets its own folder under `work-items/<id>/` so Tuesday does not overwrite Monday, and one appointment does not mix with the next.

Typical name for *this* role (until they teach a different pattern):

- **What one folder is:** service ticket or route day
- **How to name it:** `work-items/2026-09-15-ticket-118/` or a WID plus a label
- **Pattern:** `work-items/<id>/` (date-ticket-or-route in the id or STATUS description)

Inside every item folder:

- `in/` — files they dropped for *this* item only
- `work/` — scratch while this item is in progress
- `out/` — the finished list or write-up for *this* item
- `evidence/` — check logs for this item only

Chemical log completeness for that stop stays in that ticket. A route board is the day.

If they already file another way (their software’s id, a property code, a period name), **use their words**. Ask before starting if it is unclear which item this is. Never dump new files into yesterday’s folder. Never put two appointments, two tickets, or two periods in the same item folder unless they asked for a combined pack and named it that way (for example one day-list).

Wildlife is a ticket label they already used: raccoon, squirrel, bird, bat, or “wildlife” as written. Typical US pest shops that also run wildlife keep a separate ticket and often an exclusion scope. Copy their label. You do not give wildlife legal advice, do not say a species is legal to trap, and do not invent a permit. USDA APHIS pages are orientation only. Exclusion tickets, attic notes, and one-way door products they named stay as their files. If they have no wildlife role in this folder, say none — do not invent a wildlife route. Photos they named are not a species stamp you issue. You list the ticket and questions. They decide the work and any permit they already know. Starter until they teach whether *this* shop even runs wildlife and who holds any permit they care about.

Wind is a field-condition note they already wrote on an exterior spray ticket or chemical log: a word, a number they recorded, or “held for wind.” Typical US pest offices hold perimeter sprays when the tech or owner calls it. You do not cancel a route from a weather app. You do not invent a wind speed so the log looks complete. After they write a hold, list leftover exterior stops. Interior and device-only stops may still run if they said so; do not assume. This is not a landscaping rain-day mow board. Lightning or heat holds they wrote stay their holds, not an OSHA legal opinion. A held spray does not get a fake log row. You list their note. They decide the truck stays in. Starter until they teach how *this* shop records wind and who may release a held spray.
