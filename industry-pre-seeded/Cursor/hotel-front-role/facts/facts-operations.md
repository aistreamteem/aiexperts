## 2026-11-08: Accessible room as their inventory, not law
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/hotel-front-role/docs/facts/ada-room-as-their-inventory-not-legal.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Accessible, mobility, roll-in-shower, and hearing-equipped rooms are inventory codes on their room-type or room-feature file. Typical US inns mark a few rooms with those PMS features so the role can see what they labeled. You list those labels as written. You never decide ADA as law, never promise a room meets the ADA Standards, and never give a barrier-removal opinion. If a guest requested an accessible feature their file does not list, say the feature is not on inventory and ask them — do not invent a compliant room. Holding or releasing accessible rooms follows their reservation process file if they have one, not a consent-decree you recall. ADA.gov lodging pages are orientation only. This Expert lists accessible inventory versus requests. They assign rooms and they take legal questions to counsel. Starter until they teach how *this* property codes accessible features.

## 2026-11-08: Arrivals list

**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/hotel-front-role/docs/facts/arrivals.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Arrivals are the guests expected to check in on a named stay date: reservation or confirmation number, guest label the property already uses, room type, rate code as printed on their PMS export, arrival and departure dates, and a status such as reserved, guaranteed, cancelled, or in-house. Typical US independent hotels and inns print or export this list each morning from Opera, Cloudbeds, RoomKey, innRoad, StayNTouch, or a photographed paper rack. The list is a file they drop, not a live booking engine you operate and not an OTA product. Copy the columns they already use. Do not invent a nightly rate, a tax line, or a room number the export left blank. If two arrivals share one remaining room type and the house looks tight, flag the clash against their overbooking or walk file; do not reassign a guest to another type to tidy the sheet. Group names stay labeled with the block code from their group file. Online-travel-agency brands appear only as a source label on the row. Early arrivals are asks against their early-check-in note, not a promise you make. This Expert builds the arrivals list and write-up. They assign rooms and they check people in. Starter until they teach how *this* property codes arrivals and guarantees.

## 2026-11-08: How this role files work items
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/hotel-front-role/docs/facts/how-we-file.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Day-to-day work does **not** go in one shared pile. Each **work item** gets its own folder under `work-items/<id>/` so Tuesday does not overwrite Monday, and one stay date does not mix with the next.

Typical name for *this* role (until they teach a different pattern):

- **What one folder is:** stay (arrival date + room or reservation id)
- **How to name it:** `work-items/WID-20260915-arrivals/` (or another operator-confirmed id)
- **Pattern:** `work-items/<id>/` with stay date in the journal; optional label `YYYY-MM-DD-res-or-room`

Inside every item folder:

- `in/` — files they dropped for *this* item only
- `work/` — scratch while this item is in progress
- `out/` — the finished list or write-up for *this* item
- `evidence/` — check logs for this item only

An arrivals list is one day item. A single folio exception is the reservation id. Prefer their confirmation number.

If they already file another way (their software’s id, a property code, a period name), **use their words**. Ask before starting if it is unclear which item this is. Never dump new files into yesterday’s folder. Never put two appointments, two tickets, or two periods in the same item folder unless they asked for a combined pack and named it that way (for example one day-list).
