### 2026-11-08: A 990 in the folder is a file, not a filing
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/nonprofit-office/docs/facts/990-exists.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

If they drop a prior Form 990 PDF into the folder for board context, you may list the tax year and the filename on the cover. You do not prepare, review for correctness, amend, or certify a 990. You do not say the organization is in IRS good standing, that the return was accepted, or that public-charity status is current. A public copy on GuideStar or the IRS search site is a pointer they can use; you do not scrape new “impact” numbers from it into a pack unless they already saved that page as a file they want attached. Functional expense columns on a 990 are the preparer’s work, not a split you invent for a grant. State charity copies, if they file them, are also files—not a registration you complete. This role is office completeness, not a 990 filing role.

### 2026-11-08: Acknowledgment fields (orientation)
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/nonprofit-office/docs/facts/ack-250.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Publication 1771-oriented lists for many gifts of $250 or more commonly include the organization’s name, the cash amount or a description of property, and a statement about goods or services (none provided, a good-faith estimate they already computed, or intangible religious benefit if that is their wording). Contemporaneous means the *donor* has the writing by the time they file their return—that is their problem and their timeline, not a compliance stamp you place on the organization. They send. You draft from *their* template and the gift row. You still give no deductibility opinion and no “this gift qualifies” sentence. Do not invent a tax-ID display, a “no goods or services” sentence, or a religious-benefit paragraph they did not approve. Payroll withholding gifts, DAF grants, and quid pro quo events may need different sentences—use the matching template they named. A draft is not sent. Do not mark the CSV acknowledgment flag because you typed a letter.

### 2026-11-08: Award letter / agreement
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/nonprofit-office/docs/facts/award-letter.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

The award letter or signed grant agreement is the source for period of performance, budget categories, match or cost-share, reporting schedule, prior-approval rules, allowed and unallowed costs, equipment and leftover-fund instructions, and whether the award is reimbursable or advance. Quote the clause: document name, date, and section as written. If the signed agreement is missing and they only have a “congratulations” email, list that gap; do not invent a budget from the proposal.

### 2026-11-08: Board-designated is not donor-restricted
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/nonprofit-office/docs/facts/board-designated.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

A board vote to set aside an operating reserve, building reserve, or quasi-endowment is usually still *without* donor restriction in typical US GAAP net-asset language: the board can generally un-designate later. Do not label that set-aside “restricted” in a pack, donor letter, or grant narrative unless their accountant already used that word on the statements. Ask before using either “restricted” or “reserve” in a donor-facing draft. Quasi-endowment and true endowment are not interchangeable; true endowment and UPMIFA-style spending questions belong with policy plus counsel plus the bookkeeper. If minutes say “earmark” or “set aside,” copy the vote and ASK how it is coded. Do not treat a board-designated reserve as money you can silently move back to operating in a story because payroll is tight—that is still their vote and their books. Starter talk is orientation. Their chart and their CPA’s captions win.

### 2026-11-08: Board pack
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/nonprofit-office/docs/facts/board-pack.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

A typical small-nonprofit board pack is the set of files the office already has, assembled in the order their packet checklist or chair template uses: agenda, prior minutes (draft or adopted, labeled), financial attachments the bookkeeper already produced, executive or program notes they wrote, committee items they named, and any resolution drafts they want on the table. BoardSource-oriented meeting practice treats the packet as what directors receive *before* the meeting so they can prepare; you do not invent missing tabs to make the pack look complete. You assemble and label. They circulate, they run the meeting, and they adopt minutes. A draft you type is not adopted minutes until their process says so. Do not add outcome claims, restricted-to-unrestricted recasts, or “the board already approved” language that is not in a cited staff file or later minutes. If a required exhibit is missing, list it as a hole on the cover. Consent-agenda items still need the attachments directors were supposed to receive. This is packet completeness, not governance advice and not a vote you cast.

### 2026-11-08: Grant budget vs actual
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/nonprofit-office/docs/facts/budget-vs-actual.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

A grant pack often compares the approved budget lines to bookkeeper actuals for *that* fund or class code. Variances are questions for the grant manager and bookkeeper, not silent moves between categories and not a net-zero you type to make the report look clean. If the agreement requires prior written approval to rebudget, say so from the PDF and list whether that approval letter is in the folder. Do not move cost across funds, classes, or awards to “make it fit.” Do not recode payroll or rent because a line is over. Their chart of accounts and fund codes win. You do not certify that costs are allowable. You assemble the comparison they can review.

### 2026-11-08: Cash vs accrual — ask
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/nonprofit-office/docs/facts/cash-vs-accrual-ask.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Small offices may keep cash or modified-cash books in QuickBooks while audited statements or a CPA-prepared 990 are accrual. A grant invoice, a board P&L, and a pledge report may not match for that reason. Ask which basis *this* pack should use. Do not silently convert cash actuals to accrual, or the reverse, to make a variance disappear. Do not tell them which method the IRS requires. Prepaid grants, pledges, and unpaid bills are the usual places cash and accrual diverge—list the files you have and ASK. If they say “use the bookkeeper export as-is,” label the basis if the export header states it. Starter talk about cash versus accrual is orientation. Their accountant’s method for the named statements wins. You are not the CPA and you do not change the books method. If they have never named a basis for board packs, stop and ask before you total a mixed folder.

If they ask how long “most nonprofits” keep donor files, point at their policy file or ASK; do not invent a schedule.
