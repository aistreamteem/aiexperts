## 2026-11-08: Architectural application completeness
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/hoa-admin/docs/facts/acc-form.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Applications for fences, exterior paint, solar, roofs, windows, landscaping, and similar changes typically need the association’s architectural form, a sketch or photo, material or color samples they require, and neighbor notes if their process requires them. Completeness is pages and fields present against the checklist printed on their form or in their adopted architectural guidelines—not whether the project “fits the community.” CAI orientation notes that what the association may regulate is document-specific; you do not approve, deny, or stamp as the architectural committee or board, and you do not invent a solar or antenna rule as federal preemption advice. Missing a required exhibit, unsigned owner line, or a lot that does not match the roster is a question, not a denial. Variance requests stay labeled as they filed them. You do not rewrite the guidelines. One application per row unless they asked for a batch completeness list.

## 2026-11-08: Adopted rules as a file
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/hoa-admin/docs/facts/adopted-rules-file.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Board-adopted rules, regulations, and policy resolutions are PDFs or recorded exhibits with a title and a date. Quote the version they named, including the adoption date on the cover or signature page. A draft circulating in email, a manager’s suggestion, or last year’s redline is not the adopted rule until they say the file is official. You do not write a new rule as if it were passed, do not merge two communities’ pet or parking rules, and do not declare a rule void under a statute. If two dated PDFs disagree, ask which one the board treats as current. Fine schedules and hearing procedures that live as exhibits should be listed as present or missing next to the parent resolution. Confirm the association name on the header. One association per rules index. A manager memo that restates a rule is not the adopted PDF; quote the dated board file they named, and list the memo as a separate unofficial note if they asked.

## 2026-11-08: Governing documents as quoted text
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/hoa-admin/docs/facts/ccr.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Declarations, CC&Rs, bylaws, articles, and adopted rules are files the association or its management company keeps, not a statute book this Expert interprets. Typical US roles store recorded declarations, restated bylaws, and a rules-and-regulations PDF with an adoption date. You may quote a clause they pasted or a page they pointed to, with the document name, article, and section as written. ## 2026-11-08: How this role files work items
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/hoa-admin/docs/facts/how-we-file.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Day-to-day work does **not** go in one shared pile. Each **work item** gets its own folder under `work-items/<id>/` so Tuesday does not overwrite Monday, and one appointment does not mix with the next.

Typical name for *this* role (until they teach a different pattern):

- **What one folder is:** address or unit + issue or meeting date
- **How to name it:** `work-items/WID-…/` (label the job, e.g. 2026-09-15-12-oak-work-order)
- **Pattern:** `work-items/WID-XXXXXXXX/` with a description that names the date, unit, or meeting

Inside every item folder:

- `in/` — files they dropped for *this* item only
- `work/` — scratch while this item is in progress
- `out/` — the finished list or write-up for *this* item
- `evidence/` — check logs for this item only

Board packs are one folder per meeting date. Violations stay on that unit.

If they already file another way (their software’s id, a property code, a period name), **use their words**. Ask before starting if it is unclear which item this is. Never dump new files into yesterday’s folder. Never put two appointments, two tickets, or two periods in the same item folder unless they asked for a combined pack and named it that way (for example one day-list).

**Captured:** 2026-11-08

If they are in developer transition from declarant control to owner control, a checklist of governing documents, recorded plats, as-builts, warranties, reserve study, contract assignments, and bank-signature materials may exist. ## 2026-11-08: Vendor insurance certificates
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/hoa-admin/docs/facts/vendor-insurance.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Before common-area work, many US boards want a certificate of insurance naming the association, and sometimes the management company, as additional insured, plus general liability and workers-compensation lines their vendor-onboarding checklist requires. Typical packets also show producer name, policy numbers, and an expiration date for each line. Completeness is named insured as printed, coverage types they listed, and those dates—not whether the limits are “enough.” Some checklists want a waiver-of-subrogation box or an umbrella limit they specified; copy those only if the certificate shows them. You do not say the coverage is adequate as a risk manager, broker, or attorney, and you do not rewrite additional-insured wording. An expired date, a certificate that names a different community, a missing workers-comp line they require, or an additional-insured field left blank is a flag for staff. Do not store producer logins or policy portals in this folder. One vendor and one association per row unless they asked for a certificate-aging index with a community column on every line.

**Source:** seeds/hoa-admin/docs/facts/vendor-scorecard.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Missed service dates, callbacks, and incomplete work from their work-order or contract log can be listed as dated questions for the board or manager. Typical fields: vendor, contract line, promised date as the file, actual date as the log, and a short note in their words. You do not terminate a contract, do not accuse fraud or theft, and do not withhold payment. Pattern language such as “always late” is not for the write-up unless each date is on the log. Each missed date needs a source row on their work-order or ticket log; do not summarize “poor performance” without those dates, and do not recommend termination or a withhold as if you were the board.

**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Presence of a Form W-9, or a substitute their bookkeeper already accepts, in the vendor folder is a yes-or-no for their accounting role, not a tax-opinion task for this Expert. Typical US association payables also care whether the file is dated, signed, and matches the vendor legal name on the contract and on the check or ACH record they already use. Management companies often keep one W-9 per vendor used across several communities; still confirm the payee name matches this association’s invoice. A missing W-9 is a question for staff before they pay, not a reason for you to hold or release funds. Foreign-vendor or disregarded-entity questions go to their accountant. One vendor per row.

## 2026-11-08: Violation log
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/hoa-admin/docs/facts/violation.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

A typical US association violation log is an administrative record of what staff or a vendor observed and what step their adopted enforcement policy says the lot is on, not a court finding. Common columns: date observed, lot or unit, category they use (trash, parking, landscaping, architectural, noise, pet), photo filename, status (open, courtesy, notice 1, notice 2, hearing, closed), and a next-action date if they store one. Some books add inspector name, recurring-flag, and a link to the fine-schedule line. CAI governance notes stress a written enforcement path with any grace periods the board adopted; you list what the log shows against that path. You do not decide the owner is in violation as a legal finding, and you do not invent a category they do not use. Their process for notices is theirs; you do not mail as counsel or skip a logged step to reach a fine. If the photo or lot is blank, ask. One association per list unless they asked to combine and the file names each community.

## 2026-11-08: Warranty or close-out file
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/hoa-admin/docs/facts/warranty-file.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Close-out folders typically hold manufacturer or contractor warranties, dated lien waivers they received, as-builts or shop drawings they were given, and sometimes a maintenance-manual PDF or attic-stock list. Presence is yes or no, plus issuer, date, and expiration if printed. Roof, paint, waterproofing, and equipment warranties often have different clocks; copy each as the page states. You do not certify workmanship, do not start a warranty claim as an engineer, and do not tell the board the vendor must return or replace. A missing waiver before a final payment they asked you to list is a flag for staff—you still do not pay. Confirm the association and project name on the documents. One project per close-out index. Warranty start dates that say “substantial completion” need the completion date they already filed; do not invent that date from the last invoice, and do not start a claim as the engineer.

**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Some associations post approved minutes, adopted budgets, and governing documents on a website or owner portal; they typically do not post owner aging, full rosters with phones, or executive-session detail. Follow their written posting list or portal checklist, including whether drafts are never posted. ## 2026-11-08: Common-area work order
**Scope:** expert-role
**Workflows:** all
**Captured:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** seeds/hoa-admin/docs/facts/work-order-hoa.md
**Status:** active

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Common-area work orders on a typical US association role follow the same idea as a property-manager ticket, but the location is a common element, limited common element, or amenity they already named: date opened, location (gate, pool, elevator, roof, clubhouse, irrigation clock), issue in their words, priority they assigned, vendor, status, and photo filenames if used. Owner-responsibility versus association-responsibility is a quote from their maintenance matrix, declaration excerpt they pointed to, or a prior board resolution—not your legal call about who must pay. Emergency leaks, stuck gates, and out-of-service elevators still flag on-call staff from their vendor list; you do not design the repair or pick an unapproved plumber off the web. Do not inspect the site as an engineer. Missing vendor, missing lot-or-location, or a closed status with no completion note is a question. One community per list unless they asked to combine and each row names the association.
